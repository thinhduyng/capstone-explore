EXPORT AND DEDUPLICATION NOTE
-----------------------------
The three CSV files are different rankings of substantially overlapping search
results. They must not be treated as three independent samples.

- export2026.07.29-04.06.29.csv:
  patent-citation view. Every Patent Citation Count in the export is empty, so the
  view effectively falls back to title order.
- export2026.07.29-04.09.57.csv:
  paper-citation view. Article Citation Count is perfectly non-increasing, beginning
  at 139 citations.
- export2026.07.29-04.16.29.csv:
  popularity/relevance view. The ranking is neither title- nor citation-ordered, and
  IEEE does not include the popularity score in the exported CSV.

Overlap:
- 3,000 exported rows.
- 1,848 unique papers after DOI/title deduplication.
- 187 papers occur in all three exports.
- Paper-citation and popularity views overlap by 626 papers.
- Patent/title and paper-citation views overlap by 426 papers.
- Patent/title and popularity views overlap by 287 papers.

Every numbered paper below appears in at least one supplied IEEE export. Internet
search was used only to verify public artifacts and paper pages.

Artifact labels:
[A] Verified public code/data or a strong executable artifact.
[B] Partial artifact, dataset, demo, or easily runnable component.
[C] Read/method baseline; no complete official repository verified.

Ranking-view sensitivity
------------------------
The ordering changes slightly by export:

- The citation-ranked view favors established surveys, education, cybersecurity,
  software engineering, and applied systems.
- The popularity view increases software engineering and cybersecurity.
- The patent view contributes no patent-impact evidence because every exported
  patent-citation value is empty.

The deduplicated union is preferable to any single ranking, but it is still a
rank-biased sample rather than a random sample of every IEEE result.

Domain selection
----------------

Not selected:
- Healthcare: frequent but requires clinical validation and stronger safety controls.
- Business: broad and often dependent on private enterprise/customer data.
- Social/media: common, but many topics need user studies or platform data.
- Industrial engineering: viable, but ScienceDirect already supplies a stronger
  executable industrial shortlist.

Selected:
1. Secure and privacy-aware RAG.
2. Repository-grounded software-engineering automation.
3. Grounded educational/programming assistants.


PART 2: SECURE AND PRIVACY-AWARE RAG
====================================

Recommended thesis shape
------------------------
Use one controlled RAG corpus and evaluate privacy, poisoning, or indirect prompt
injection. The most manageable extension is cross-dataset detection or provenance,
not inventing a completely new attack. Run experiments only against team-owned or
explicitly authorized systems.

1. [A] AttnTrace: Contextual Attribution of Prompt Injection and Knowledge
   Corruption

   Venue/year: IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/SP63933.2026.00198
   Code/data: https://github.com/Wang-Yanting/AttnTrace

   Why use:
   - Attributes malicious outputs to responsible context spans.
   - Covers indirect prompt injection and poisoned knowledge.
   - Includes code, data, and an executable notebook.

2. [A] DrainCode: Stealthy Energy Consumption Attacks on
   Retrieval-Augmented Code Generation via Context Poisoning

   Venue/year: IEEE/ACM ASE, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ASE63991.2025.00070
   Code: https://github.com/DeepSoftwareAnalytics/DrainCode

   Why use:
   - Connects RAG poisoning to code generation and energy/resource consumption.
   - Provides a useful bridge between the security and software recommendations.
   - Supports measurable attack success, code behavior, latency, and resource cost.

3. [A] DarkCite: Unveiling Authority Bias as Implicit RAG Jailbreak Attacks

   Venue/year: IEEE ICASSP, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICASSP55912.2026.11463457
   Code: https://github.com/YancyKahn/DarkCite

   Why use:
   - Studies authority/citation cues as an implicit jailbreak mechanism.
   - Appropriate for a thesis on evidence provenance and citation trust.
   - Can be tested safely with synthetic documents in a private corpus.

4. [C, privacy baseline] Generating Is Believing: Membership Inference
   Attacks against Retrieval-Augmented Generation

   Method: S2MIA
   Venue/year: IEEE ICASSP, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICASSP49660.2025.10889013
   Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19234
   IEEE paper citations in export: 12

   Why read:
   - Tests whether an attacker can infer if a document belongs to a RAG corpus.
   - Focuses on database privacy rather than only generator privacy.
   - Gives a different security axis from corpus poisoning.

5. [C] Securing RAG: A Risk Assessment and Mitigation Framework

   Venue/year: IEEE SDS, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/SDS66131.2025.00024
   IEEE paper citations in export: 10

   Why read:
   - Useful for defining threat actors, system assets, failure points, and controls.
   - Helps prevent an experiment from mixing incompatible attack assumptions.

6. [C, evaluation baseline] RAGProbe: Breaking RAG Pipelines with Evaluation
   Scenarios

   Venue/year: IEEE/ACM CAIN, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/CAIN66642.2025.00015

   Why use:
   - Treats adversarial evaluation as a software-quality problem.
   - Generates failure-triggering evaluation scenarios for RAG pipelines.
   - Suitable for regression testing in a CI/CD workflow.

Minimum defensible security experiment
--------------------------------------
- Explicit attacker knowledge and corpus access assumptions.
- At least two attacks or attack variants.
- No-defense, simple-filter, and one released defense/attribution baseline.
- Two public QA datasets plus one small Vietnamese or code corpus.
- Attack success, clean accuracy, poison retrieval, detector precision/recall/F1,
  false positives, latency, token cost, and resource/energy cost where applicable.
- Adaptive or paraphrased attacks held out from tuning.


PART 3: REPOSITORY-GROUNDED SOFTWARE-ENGINEERING AUTOMATION
===========================================================

Recommended thesis shape
------------------------
Choose one automatically verifiable task: repository summarization, requirements,
commit messages, secure code generation, or test generation. A strong capstone
compares flat semantic retrieval with AST/dependency/task-aware retrieval.

1. [A] Code Summarization Beyond Function Level

   Venue/year: IEEE/ACM LLM4Code, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/LLM4Code66737.2025.00024
   Code/data/results:
   https://github.com/kilimanj4r0/code-summarization-beyond-function-level
   IEEE paper citations in export: 13

   Why use:
   - Compares function, class, and repository context.
   - Releases code, datasets, and evaluation outputs.
   - Includes RAG and smaller code-model baselines.

2. [A] Accelerating Requirements Specification Using Large Language Models
   and Retrieval Augmented Generation – A Case Study

   Venue/year: IEEE MELECON, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/MELECON64486.2026.11418833
   Code: https://github.com/frickly-systems/reqrag

   Why use:
   - Provides a direct requirements-engineering RAG implementation.
   - Appropriate for testing traceability, completeness, and project terminology.

3. [A] MCCoder: Streamlining Motion Control with LLM-Assisted Code
   Generation and Rigorous Verification

   Venue/year: IEEE CASE, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/CASE58245.2025.11163835
   Code: https://github.com/MCCodeAI/MCCoder

   Why use:
   - Couples generation with explicit verification.
   - Demonstrates why execution/validation matters more than textual similarity.
   - Use its architecture on a safe simulator rather than physical motion hardware.

4. [A] SAGE-HLS: Syntax-Aware AST-Guided LLM for High-Level Synthesis Code
   Generation

   Venue/year: IEEE ICCD, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCD65941.2025.00088
   Code: https://github.com/zfsadik/SAGEHLS

   Why use:
   - Incorporates syntax/AST structure into code generation.
   - Provides a strong structure-aware comparison to plain vector retrieval.

5. [C] Enhancing Commit Message Generation in Software Repositories:
   A RAG-Based Approach

   Venue/year: IEEE SERA, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/SERA65747.2025.11154546

   Why use:
   - Simple task with naturally available public Git histories.
   - Can compare repository-local versus cross-repository retrieval.
   - Evaluation should include factual correctness and usefulness, not BLEU alone.

6. [C] Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Software Requirement-Based Test
   Case Generation

   Venue/year: IEEE QRS, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/QRS65678.2025.00022

   Why use:
   - Connects requirements, retrieval, and executable tests.
   - Provides a natural extension using traceability-aware context selection.

Minimum defensible software experiment
--------------------------------------
- Freeze repository commits and prevent temporal/test leakage.
- Baselines: no retrieval, BM25, dense, hybrid.
- Proposed method: AST/dependency/test/requirement-aware retrieval or reranking.
- Retrieval metrics: Recall@k, MRR/nDCG, context tokens.
- Task metrics: compilation, tests passed, pass@k, coverage/mutation score, exact
  trace recovery, or factual summary quality.
- Report latency, token/API cost, and one small open model.


PART 4: GROUNDED EDUCATIONAL AND PROGRAMMING ASSISTANTS
=======================================================

Recommended thesis shape
------------------------
Use one HCMUS course with public or instructor-authorized materials. Separate
retrieval correctness from answer quality and learning outcomes. If a full student
study is infeasible, evaluate retrieval, factuality, hint quality, and instructor
judgment without claiming improved learning.

1. [C, essential empirical baseline] An LLM-Driven Chatbot in Higher
   Education for Databases and Information Systems

   System: MoodleBot
   Venue/year: IEEE Transactions on Education, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TE.2024.3467912
   IEEE paper citations in export: 139
   Popularity-view rank: 1

   Why read:
   - Most cited and most popular paper across the supplied IEEE views.
   - Deploys a course-grounded RAG chatbot in a real computer-science classroom.
   - Evaluates answer accuracy and student technology acceptance.
   - Provides a realistic study-design baseline, even without a verified repository.

2. [A/B] KAG: A Scalable Knowledge-Augmented Generation System for
   Educational Content Management

   Venue/year: IEEE FLLM, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/FLLM67465.2025.11391105
   Code/resources: https://github.com/Hadi-Hsn/KAG

   Why use:
   - Public implementation for educational knowledge ingestion and retrieval.
   - Focuses on scalable content management and source-grounded answers.

3. [B, bilingual dataset] Adaptive Chunking for VideoRAG Pipelines with a
   Newly Gathered Bilingual Educational Dataset

   Venue/year: IEEE CSICC, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/CSICC65765.2025.10967455
   Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/uIAICIEduViQA

   Why use:
   - Supplies a concrete bilingual educational VideoRAG dataset.
   - Useful for testing adaptive versus fixed chunking.
   - Demonstrates that Hugging Face is valuable as an artifact source even when its
     paper search returns few results.

4. [A/B] Multimodal AI for Romanian University Support: An LLM, RAG and
   Voice Approach

   Venue/year: IEEE SACI, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/SACI66288.2025.11030141
   Code: https://github.com/Andrei481/RomanianChatbot

   Why use:
   - Released multilingual university-support implementation.
   - A useful localization baseline for Vietnamese institutional information.

5. [C] Optimizing RAG in Programming Education: A Comparative Study of
   Models and Strategies

   Venue/year: IEEE Access, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3629843
   Popularity-view rank: 71

   Why use:
   - Directly addresses programming education.
   - Useful experimental comparison for models, retrieval, and prompt strategies.

6. [C] How to Build an Adaptive AI Tutor for Any Course Using Knowledge
   Graph-Enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation

   Venue/year: IEEE ICEIT, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICEIT64364.2025.10975937
   IEEE paper citations in export: 15

   Why use:
   - Adds course knowledge graphs and adaptive tutoring to standard RAG.
   - Useful architecture reference, but a team should first prove a simpler RAG
     baseline and define measurable pedagogy.

Minimum defensible educational experiment
-----------------------------------------
- One course and a versioned, authorized corpus.
- BM25, dense, hybrid, and reranked retrieval.
- Fixed, semantic, and curriculum/section-aware chunking.
- Recall@k/MRR, citation correctness, factuality, hint correctness, unsupported
  claims, latency, and cost.
- Instructor-reviewed questions and answers.
- If conducting a student study: consent, predefined outcomes, comparison condition,
  and sufficient sample size.


PART 5: FINAL IEEE DECISION
===========================

Best execution choice:
Repository-grounded software engineering, because released code and automatic
correctness metrics reduce dependence on outside experts.

Best mature research choice:
Secure RAG, because IEEE supplies privacy, poisoning, attribution, code-security,
and risk/evaluation papers.

Best deployed-system choice:
Educational/programming RAG, because MoodleBot provides unusually strong citation
and real-classroom evidence, while KAG and bilingual artifacts provide implementation
starting points.

Legal RAG remains a strong local alternative. It ranks eighth in the harmonized IEEE
union, and the citation/popularity views contain influential legal surveys and
systems. However, the ScienceDirect collection currently provides the more directly
relevant Vietnamese legal-retrieval baseline.

PAPER SHORTLIST FOR THREE FEASIBLE CAPSTONE DIRECTIONS
=====================================================

Prepared from explore/SearchResults.csv, with public artifact availability checked
on 2026-07-29.

Assumptions:
- The team is primarily computer-science students.
- The project lasts one or two semesters.
- Commodity hardware, a modest GPU, or limited API credits are available.
- The team does not have privileged legal, educational, or security data.

Artifact labels:
[A] Best baseline: public code plus usable data/model/notebook.
[B] Partial baseline: public data or model, but no verified complete implementation.
[C] Read: valuable method/evaluation paper, but no verified public reproduction package.

Important: "public" does not mean unrestricted. Check each repository/dataset license
before redistributing data or releasing a derivative system.


RECOMMENDATION 1: CITATION-GROUNDED VIETNAMESE LEGAL RAG
=======================================================

Best starting combination
-------------------------
Use paper 1 as the executable retrieval/citation baseline, paper 2 for a lightweight
BM25-versus-dense retrieval experiment, and paper 5 to design a broader legal
evaluation. Replace the Australian/Pakistani corpus with Vietnamese legislation and
add temporal validity and citation-faithfulness tests.

1. [A] Legal Citation Prediction with LLMs: A Comparative Evaluation of
   Instruction Tuning, Retrieval, and Jurisdiction-Specific Pre-training on the
   AusLaw Citation Benchmark

   Authors: Jiuzhou Han, Paul Burgess, Ehsan Shareghi
   Venue/year: Artificial Intelligence and Law, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10506-026-09506-9
   Project: https://auslawbench.github.io/
   Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/auslawbench/AusLaw-Citation-Benchmark
   Models: https://huggingface.co/auslawbench
   Runnable notebook:
   https://colab.research.google.com/drive/1N3DR3xbN8HoGuzE39kCpYXB4qyPjk9gt?usp=sharing

   Why it is the best baseline:
   - Supplies 55k real-world examples, a 1k test split, models, and notebook code.
   - Directly compares prompting, BM25/dense retrieval, fine-tuning, and reranking.
   - Shows that standalone generation is a weak legal-citation baseline and that
     hybrid retrieval/reranking performs best.

   What to reproduce:
   - BM25, dense retrieval, and hybrid/reranked retrieval.
   - Recall@k/MRR or the paper's retrieval metrics.
   - Exact citation accuracy and explanations for why a source should be cited.

   Thesis extension:
   Create a Vietnamese statute/citation benchmark, compare multilingual and
   Vietnamese embeddings, and require every generated answer to point to the exact
   article/paragraph that supports it.

2. [C, easy to reimplement] Semantic Clause Retrieval for Trademark Law Using
   Transformer Encoders and Lexical Baselines: A Cross-Domain Agri-Robotics
   Compliance Case Study

   Authors: Muhammad Asfand E Yar et al.
   Venue/year: Scientific Reports, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-43098-3

   Why read/use:
   - A small, understandable clause-level retrieval baseline.
   - Compares TF-IDF, BM25, and off-the-shelf sentence encoders with cosine
     similarity.
   - Uses AP/MAP and expert relevance judgments rather than only answer fluency.
   - Reported methodology is simple enough to rebuild with rank_bm25,
     sentence-transformers, and FAISS even though no official repository was found.

   Limitation:
   The main trademark evaluation has only ten practitioner-style queries, so do not
   treat its reported scores as a robust general benchmark.

3. [B/C] Neural Reranking for UK Statutory Retrieval: Provision-Level Evaluation
   and an Open Distilled Model

   Authors: Alshehri et al.
   Venue/year: Artificial Intelligence and Law, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10506-025-09501-6
   Accessible manuscript:
   https://adaptive-intelligence.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/alshehri2026neural.pdf

   Why read/use:
   - Compares BM25 and MPNet first-stage retrieval followed by neural reranking.
   - Uses 100 queries with graded provision-level relevance judgments.
   - Describes corpus extraction/preprocessing and an open distilled reranker.

   Availability warning:
   The accessible double-blind manuscript says the repository link was omitted.
   Confirm the final publisher page/model location before selecting it as the team's
   only executable baseline.

4. [C] LegalAI Research in the LLM Era: Data, Modeling and Evaluation

   Venue/year: Artificial Intelligence Review, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10462-026-11514-9

   Why read:
   Use this survey to map the field, choose a legal task, identify datasets/models,
   and avoid presenting a generic chatbot as a research contribution.

5. [A, external supporting baseline] LegalBench: A Collaboratively Built Benchmark
   for Measuring Legal Reasoning in Large Language Models

   Authors: Neel Guha et al.
   Year: 2023
   Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11462
   Code/data: https://github.com/HazyResearch/legalbench

   Why use:
   - Contains 162 tasks contributed by legal and technical experts.
   - Includes datasets, prompt configurations, notebook, and evaluation code.
   - Useful for designing a Vietnamese legal benchmark that measures extraction,
     rule application, interpretation, and issue spotting separately.

6. [A, external supporting baseline] LexGLUE: A Benchmark Dataset for Legal
   Language Understanding in English

   Authors: Ilias Chalkidis et al.
   Venue/year: ACL, 2022
   Paper: https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.297/
   Code/data: https://github.com/coastalcph/lex-glue

   Why use:
   A mature legal-NLP benchmark and training codebase. It is more suitable than
   LegalBench when the thesis focuses on classification or retrieval encoders rather
   than end-to-end generative RAG.

Useful Vietnamese resources (resources, not substitutes for peer-reviewed baselines)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Reproducible Vietnamese legal-document/instruction dataset generator:
  https://github.com/duyet/vietnamese-legal-documents-dataset
- Vietnamese legal QA data card:
  https://huggingface.co/datasets/kiethuynhanh/vnlegal-dataset
- Related Vietnamese paper: Answering Legal Questions by Learning Neural Attentive
  Text Representation (COLING 2020):
  https://aclanthology.org/2020.coling-main.86/

Minimum defensible experiment
-----------------------------
- Corpus: one bounded Vietnamese legal area, with document version/effective dates.
- Baselines: BM25; one multilingual dense retriever; hybrid BM25+dense; one reranker.
- RAG variants: retrieval-only and retrieval-plus-generation.
- Metrics: Recall@5/10, MRR or nDCG, exact article match, citation precision,
  citation entailment/faithfulness, answer correctness, latency, and cost.
- Evaluation set: at least 100 manually reviewed questions with answer passages.
- Safety: label the system as research assistance, not legal advice.


RECOMMENDATION 2: CURRICULUM-GROUNDED EDUCATIONAL TUTOR
======================================================

Best starting combination
-------------------------
Use MathDial or SocraticMath for a working dialogue/evaluation baseline, then adapt
the RAG pipeline and evaluation dimensions from paper 1 below to a Vietnamese HCMUS
course. Paper 2 is valuable for measuring actual learning rather than merely chatbot
quality.

1. [B, public source corpus] Evaluating Large Language Models in
   Retrieval-Augmented Tutoring Systems: Methods and Emerging Tools

   Authors: Owais Bhat et al.
   Venue/year: SN Computer Science, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s42979-026-04789-w
   Public textbook source:
   https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php?hehd1=1-10

   Why use:
   - Closest paper in SearchResults.csv to an executable curriculum-grounded tutor.
   - Compares retrieval/generation configurations and semantic reranking.
   - Evaluates Precision@K, Recall@K, BLEU, ROUGE-L, and BERTScore.
   - Uses public textbook PDFs, so its data-collection setup can be recreated.

   Availability warning:
   No official reproduction repository was found. Treat the architecture as a
   reference and implement a clean baseline using a standard RAG stack.

2. [C] The Impact of an LLM-Based Educational Agent on Learning Achievement,
   Cognitive Dynamics, and Student Perceptions in Computer Science Education

   Venue/year: International Journal of STEM Education, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00641-y

   Why read:
   - Evaluates DBagent in real database-programming learning tasks.
   - Measures learning performance, cognitive engagement, and student perceptions.
   - Helps the team design a user study rather than reporting only automatic NLP
     metrics.

   Availability warning:
   The dataset is available only from the authors on reasonable request, and no
   public code repository was found.

3. [C, prompts available] What Don't You Understand? Using Large Language Models
   to Identify and Characterize Student Misconceptions About Challenging Topics

   Authors: Michael J. Parker, Maria G. Zavala-Cerna
   Venue/year: Education and Information Technologies, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-026-13902-8

   Why read/use:
   - Combines quiz performance, response patterns, and lecture transcripts to locate
     and explain misconceptions.
   - Includes its prompts and expert-rating rubric in the appendix.
   - Provides a strong extension: have a tutor retrieve course evidence and respond
     differently according to the detected misconception.

   Availability warning:
   Quiz questions are intentionally not public; other data require author and ethics
   approval. Recreate the protocol on team-owned course material instead.

4. [A, strongest runnable education baseline] MathDial: A Dialogue Tutoring Dataset
   with Rich Pedagogical Properties Grounded in Math Reasoning Problems

   Authors: Jakub Macina et al.
   Venue/year: Findings of EMNLP, 2023
   Code/data/models: https://github.com/eth-nlped/mathdial

   Why it is an excellent baseline:
   - Includes 2,861 tutoring conversations with student confusions and pedagogical
     dialogue-act labels.
   - Includes train/test data, generation code, interactive evaluation, and supervised
     fine-tuning.
   - Provides a runnable Qwen2.5-1.5B tutor model, making it feasible on modest
     hardware.
   - Measures the important trade-off between helping a learner and revealing the
     solution too early.

   Thesis extension:
   Add retrieval from Vietnamese course notes, support programming/database
   misconceptions, and compare generic RAG with pedagogy-constrained RAG.

5. [A] Boosting Large Language Models with Socratic Method for Conversational
   Mathematics Teaching (SocraticMath)

   Venue/year: CIKM, 2024
   Code/data: https://github.com/ECNU-ICALK/SocraticMath

   Why use:
   - Official implementation and a dataset of 6,846 Socratic tutoring conversations.
   - Gives a concrete dialogue strategy: review, heuristic guidance, rectification,
     and summarization.
   - A good baseline for evaluating whether the system teaches rather than simply
     answers.

6. [A] EduChat: A Large-Scale Language Model-Based Chatbot System for Intelligent
   Education

   Venue/year: CCKS, 2024
   Paper/model/code: https://github.com/ECNU-ICALK/EduChat

   Why use:
   Provides an open educational chat model, data-processing scripts, local deployment,
   Socratic teaching, essay feedback, and emotional-support modes. It is a useful
   working system reference, although its Chinese/English focus requires adaptation.

Optional dataset for feedback generation
----------------------------------------
- MathEDU: authentic student solutions plus expert feedback annotations:
  https://github.com/NYCU-NLP-Lab/MathEDU

Minimum defensible experiment
-----------------------------
- Course scope: one subject, such as Database Systems or Introductory Programming.
- Baselines: plain LLM; naive RAG; reranked RAG; pedagogy-constrained reranked RAG.
- Retrieval metrics: Recall@k, MRR/nDCG.
- Answer metrics: correctness, groundedness/citation accuracy, hint quality, and
  solution-leakage rate.
- Educational metrics: pre/post quiz improvement or misconception correction, plus
  a small blinded instructor rating.
- Keep any student data anonymized and obtain the required institutional/participant
  approval before a user study.


RECOMMENDATION 3: SECURE RAG / PROMPT-INJECTION DETECTION
========================================================

Best starting combination
-------------------------
Start with paper 1 because its exact code is public and its classifiers are affordable.
Use paper 2 or 3 to add realistic indirect/document-based attacks. Add SAFE-CACHE
only as a second-stage extension because its full data/code are not public.

1. [A, recommended primary baseline] Comparative Evaluation of Machine Learning
   Methods for Protecting LLMs from Prompt Injection Attacks

   Venue/year: International Journal of Information Security, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10207-026-01264-8
   Replication package:
   https://github.com/toyhtowdomasydyt/llm-prompt-injection-detector

   Why it is the best baseline:
   - Compares Naive Bayes, LightGBM, Random Forest, MLP, fine-tuned DeBERTa,
     DistilBERT and RoBERTa, specialized detectors, and LLM classifiers.
   - Uses one consistent test set and reports accuracy, precision, recall, and F1.
   - Traditional models are cheap and fast enough for a student project.
   - The paper explicitly identifies cross-dataset generalization and hybrid
     detection as open problems.

   Thesis extension:
   Evaluate cross-dataset and Vietnamese/multilingual generalization; construct a
   two-stage low-latency detector; or measure detector utility inside a real RAG
   pipeline rather than as isolated text classification.

2. [A] Exploiting Large Language Models in Peer Review: Indirect Prompt Injection
   Attacks and Integrity Probes

   Venue/year: Scientometrics, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-026-05695-x
   Code/data: https://github.com/federicotorrielli/indirect-prompt-injection

   Why use:
   - Fully reproducible document/PDF-based indirect-injection pipeline.
   - Includes upload validation, retry logic, repeated trials, and an experiment
     dataset.
   - Demonstrates an attack delivered through retrieved or uploaded content, which
     is much closer to the threat model of RAG than direct jailbreak prompts.

   Safe-use note:
   Run only against systems/accounts that the team owns or is explicitly authorized
   to test.

3. [B, public dataset] Indirect Prompt Injection in Large Language Models

   Venue/year: Neural Computing and Applications, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-026-12266-x
   Dataset named by the paper: Indirect Prompt Injection CV Dataset

   Why use:
   - Supplies realistic malicious CV/document scenarios.
   - Separates detection from mitigation and reports attack success/bias effects.
   - Useful for testing whether a malicious document can influence evaluation of
     other clean documents.

   Availability warning:
   The publisher page points to a public IEEE DataPort/Dataverse resource, but no
   complete official code repository was found. Verify download terms before making
   it the primary dataset.

4. [B] Enhancing Adversarial Resilience in Semantic Caching for Secure
   Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems

   Authors: Mohanad Afiffy, Mohamed Waleed Fakhr, Fahima A. Maghraby
   Venue/year: Scientific Reports, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-36721-w
   Open model:
   https://huggingface.co/mohanadffy/gemma3-intent-paraphraser-lora

   Why read/use:
   - Introduces SAFE-CACHE, using cluster centroids instead of single-query nearest
     neighbors to resist cache poisoning.
   - Evaluates cache precision, attack success rate, and utility/security trade-offs.
   - Provides a fine-tuned Gemma-3 1B paraphraser that can run on modest hardware.

   Availability warning:
   The full datasets are available only on request and no official code repository
   was found. Rebuild only after the prompt-injection detector baseline works.

5. [A, external supporting baseline] Embedding-Based Detection of Indirect Prompt
   Injection Attacks in Large Language Models Using Semantic Context Analysis

   Venue/year: Algorithms, 2026
   Paper: https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4893/19/1/92
   Code:
   https://github.com/Abu-Hussain/Embedding-Based-Detection-of-Indirect-Prompt-Injection-Attacks-in-Large-Language-Models
   Dataset:
   https://huggingface.co/datasets/MAlmasabi/Indirect-Prompt-Injection-BIPIA-GPT

   Why use:
   - Public preprocessing, embedding extraction, classifier training, and evaluation.
   - Model-agnostic and directly targets indirect prompt injection.
   - The extended BIPIA dataset enables a valuable cross-dataset test against the
     replication package in paper 1.

6. [C, survey] End-to-End Security Threats and Defenses in Retrieval-Augmented
   LLM Agents

   Venue/year: Discover Artificial Intelligence, 2026
   DOI/paper: https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-01726-x

   Why read:
   Use its threat taxonomy to define the thesis scope and distinguish corpus
   poisoning, indirect prompt injection, retriever manipulation, insecure tool use,
   and output attacks. Do not attempt to solve every layer in one capstone.

Minimum defensible experiment
-----------------------------
- Threat model: malicious instructions embedded in documents retrieved by a RAG
  system; no attacks against third-party systems.
- Baselines: rules; TF-IDF/logistic regression; one tree model; one small transformer;
  one available specialized detector.
- Data protocol: in-domain split plus genuinely separate cross-dataset evaluation.
- Metrics: precision, recall, F1, false-positive rate, attack success rate after
  defense, clean-task utility, latency, and memory/cost.
- Required ablations: detector threshold; obfuscation/paraphrase types; direct versus
  indirect attacks; with/without context; English versus Vietnamese.
- A strong thesis result may be a carefully measured negative finding—for example,
  high in-dataset F1 but poor cross-dataset recall—followed by a reproducible hybrid
  defense that improves robustness without unacceptable false positives.


OVERALL PRIORITY FOR THE TEAM
=============================

1. Secure RAG / prompt-injection detection
   Best artifact availability and easiest fully reproducible baseline. Start from the
   llm-prompt-injection-detector repository and add cross-dataset or Vietnamese tests.

2. Vietnamese legal RAG
   Strongest local relevance. Start from AusLaw's benchmark/notebook and build a
   narrowly scoped, expert-reviewed Vietnamese retrieval/citation dataset.

3. Educational tutor
   Strong practical impact but a credible learning-effect claim requires a user study.
   Start from MathDial/SocraticMath and keep the initial course scope small.

Avoid making "we built a chatbot" the contribution. In all three directions, the
research contribution should be a dataset, evaluation protocol, measured failure
mode, retrieval/defense improvement, or carefully validated system design.

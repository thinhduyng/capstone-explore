IMPORTANT SOURCE NOTE
---------------------
Every numbered paper in this file appears in the supplied Scopus CSV. Internet
search was used only to verify public repositories, datasets, models, project pages,
and paper URLs. No externally discovered paper was added to the numbered shortlist.

Dataset overview:
- 8,000 Scopus records.
- Publication years: 4,228 from 2025; 3,730 from 2026; 42 indexed as 2027.
- 5,028 conference papers and 2,474 journal articles, plus smaller document types.
- Every record has an abstract; 6,540 have author keywords.

Assumptions:
- The team is primarily computer-science students.
- The thesis lasts one or two semesters.
- The team has commodity hardware, a modest GPU, or limited API credits.
- The team does not have privileged clinical or corporate data.

Artifact labels:
[A] Strong executable baseline: verified public code plus data/evaluation assets.
[B] Working implementation: verified public system/code, but reproduction assets may
    be incomplete or require team-created data.
[C] Read/method baseline: useful experimental design, but no verified full repository.

Always check repository and dataset licenses before redistribution.


PART 1: FIVE MOST COMMON APPLICATION DOMAINS
============================================

Method
------
The CSV has no application-domain column. Domains were therefore estimated using
high-precision keyword rules over each paper's title and author keywords. The counts
are approximate and multi-label: one paper may belong to more than one domain.
Abstracts were reviewed when selecting papers but were not used for raw domain counts,
because background statements in abstracts create too many false positives.

Rank  Domain                         Approx. papers   Prevalence   Feasibility
----  -----------------------------  --------------   ----------   -----------
1     Healthcare and biomedicine     1,214            15.2%        2/5
2     Education and learning           628             7.8%        5/5
3     Business and finance              415             5.2%        3/5
4     Cybersecurity and privacy         353             4.4%        5/5
5     Law, policy and government        340             4.2%        4/5

Interpretation and decision
---------------------------

1. Healthcare and biomedicine
   Most common, but not selected. A credible clinical thesis frequently requires
   protected data, ethics approval, medical expertise, and specialist validation.
   It remains possible only if the team already has a medical collaborator and a
   bounded public benchmark.

2. Education and learning
   SELECTED. Course materials are obtainable, HCMUS students/instructors are reachable
   for evaluation, and retrieval accuracy, groundedness, hint quality, and learning
   outcomes can be measured. Several Scopus papers have working open-source systems.

3. Business and finance
   Not selected for the primary shortlist. The category is broad and many convincing
   applications depend on private customer, accounting, or enterprise documents.
   It becomes attractive if the team has a real company partner and permission to use
   representative data.

4. Cybersecurity and privacy
   SELECTED. Scopus contains mature attack and defense baselines with public code,
   standard QA datasets, and objective metrics such as attack success rate, accuracy,
   false-positive rate, latency, and clean-task utility.

5. Law, policy and government
   SELECTED. Legal documents are textual and often public; retrieval and citation
   metrics are clear; and the Scopus export contains strong open legal RAG toolkits.
   Vietnamese localization offers a defensible contribution, provided legal experts
   review the evaluation set and outputs are not presented as legal advice.

Final three recommendations:
1. Secure RAG against corpus poisoning and indirect prompt injection.
2. Citation-grounded Vietnamese legal RAG.
3. Curriculum-grounded educational RAG/tutoring.


PART 2: SECURE RAG AGAINST POISONING AND PROMPT INJECTION
=========================================================

Recommended starting combination
--------------------------------
Use PoisonedRAG as the attack baseline and RAGDefender as the primary defense. Add
AttnTrace if the thesis focuses on locating the malicious passage, or RAG-SAGE if it
focuses on privacy rather than integrity. Restrict all experiments to systems and
datasets the team owns or is explicitly authorized to test.

1. [A] PoisonedRAG: Knowledge Corruption Attacks to Retrieval-Augmented
   Generation of Large Language Models

   Venue/year: USENIX Security, 2025
   Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07867
   Official code: https://github.com/sleeepeer/PoisonedRAG
   Scopus "Cited by" value in export: 49

   Why it is the essential attack baseline:
   - Defines a concrete corpus-poisoning threat model for RAG.
   - Provides code for adversarial-passage generation and evaluation.
   - Uses standard datasets including Natural Questions, MS MARCO, and HotpotQA.
   - Measures whether a few injected passages can force target answers.

   What to reproduce:
   - Clean answer accuracy/exact match.
   - Retrieval rate of poisoned passages.
   - End-to-end attack success rate (ASR).
   - Results across at least two retrievers and two generators.

   Feasible extension:
   Test transfer to Vietnamese documents and queries, or build a cross-dataset
   evaluation showing whether defenses tuned on English QA generalize.

2. [A, recommended primary defense] Rescuing the Unpoisoned: Efficient Defense
   Against Knowledge Corruption Attacks on RAG Systems

   Venue/year: ACSAC, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ACSAC67867.2025.00093
   Code/artifacts: https://github.com/SecAI-Lab/RAGDefender
   Installable package: https://pypi.org/project/ragdefender/

   Why it is the best student-team defense baseline:
   - Filters adversarial passages after retrieval without additional model training.
   - Repository includes datasets, poisoned corpora, scripts, expected outputs, and
     separate reproduction claims.
   - Directly supports PoisonedRAG and multiple other attack settings.
   - Easier to modify and ablate than a large end-to-end trained defense.

   Thesis extensions:
   - Add multilingual features or Vietnamese embedding models.
   - Compare clustering/concentration features against a small transformer detector.
   - Optimize ASR, clean accuracy, false positives, and latency jointly.

3. [A] AttnTrace: Contextual Attribution of Prompt Injection and Knowledge
   Corruption

   Venue/year: IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/SP63933.2026.00198
   Code/data/notebook: https://github.com/Wang-Yanting/AttnTrace

   Why use:
   - Locates text spans responsible for an LLM output before classifying an attack.
   - Includes a quick-start notebook and scripts for LongBench prompt injection and
     PoisonedRAG knowledge corruption.
   - Covers long-context attacks such as malicious instructions hidden in papers,
     code, or agent memory.

   Feasibility warning:
   The reference configuration uses Llama 3.1 8B and attention access. Begin with a
   small subset and one open-weight model before scaling.

4. [A] Traceback of Poisoning Attacks to Retrieval-Augmented Generation

   Method/system: RAGForensics
   Venue/year: ACM Web Conference, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3696410.3714756
   Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21668
   Code: https://github.com/zhangbl6618/RAG-Responsibility-Attribution
   Scopus "Cited by" value in export: 12

   Why use:
   - Changes the problem from only detecting a bad answer to tracing which stored
     passages caused it.
   - Evaluates against several poisoning attacks and datasets.
   - Useful if the thesis contribution is incident investigation, provenance, or
     knowledge-base cleanup.

5. [A] Mitigating the Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
   via Pure Synthetic Data

   Method: RAG-SAGE
   Venue/year: EMNLP, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1247
   Code: https://github.com/phycholosogy/RAG-SAGE
   Scopus "Cited by" value in export: 8

   Why use:
   - Gives the team a second secure-RAG axis: privacy leakage rather than poisoning.
   - Public code permits comparison of private-source RAG with synthetic-data
     alternatives.
   - Appropriate if the thesis focuses on sensitive institutional documents.

6. [A] Beyond Text: Unveiling Privacy Vulnerabilities in Multi-modal
   Retrieval-Augmented Generation

   Venue/year: EMNLP, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1259
   Code: https://github.com/phycholosogy/MRAG-privacy

   Why use:
   - Extends privacy attacks from text-only RAG to multimodal retrieval.
   - Provides an advanced follow-on experiment after a text-only baseline works.
   - Relevant if the team wants to study PDFs, screenshots, slides, or document
     images rather than plain text.

Minimum defensible secure-RAG experiment
---------------------------------------
- Threat model: an attacker can add or modify documents in the retrieval corpus but
  cannot modify the model or defense.
- Baselines: no defense; similarity/perplexity filter; RAGDefender; one proposed
  hybrid or multilingual defense.
- Data: at least two public QA datasets plus a bounded Vietnamese test collection.
- Metrics: ASR, clean exact match/accuracy, poisoned-document retrieval rate,
  precision/recall/F1 of poison detection, false-positive rate, latency, and cost.
- Evaluation: include adaptive or paraphrased attacks not seen during tuning.
- Safety: do not target third-party RAG services, accounts, or knowledge bases.


PART 3: CITATION-GROUNDED VIETNAMESE LEGAL RAG
==============================================

Recommended starting combination
--------------------------------
Use LexRAG as the main end-to-end toolkit and AusLaw as the citation-prediction
baseline. Add HCLeK for long legal documents or ScenarioBench for auditable,
clause-grounded evaluation. Replace the foreign corpus with one bounded Vietnamese
legal area and preserve document versions/effective dates.

1. [A, recommended end-to-end baseline] LexRAG: Benchmarking
   Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Multi-Turn Legal Consultation Conversation

   Venue/year: ACM SIGIR, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3726302.3730340
   Code/data/toolkit: https://github.com/CSHaitao/LexRAG
   Scopus "Cited by" value in export: 9

   Why it is the strongest overall baseline:
   - Includes 1,013 five-turn legal conversations.
   - Includes a corpus of 17,228 Chinese statutory provisions.
   - Supports query rewriting, BM25/QLD, dense retrieval, FAISS, local or API
     generators, and modular prompt builders.
   - Provides retrieval metrics (Recall, Precision, F1, nDCG, MRR), generation
     metrics, and an LLM-as-judge implementation.

   Thesis extension:
   Build a Vietnamese multi-turn legal consultation benchmark and test whether
   conversation-aware query rewriting improves exact article retrieval without
   increasing unsupported claims.

2. [A, recommended citation baseline] Legal Citation Prediction with LLMs:
   A Comparative Evaluation of Instruction Tuning, Retrieval, and
   Jurisdiction-Specific Pre-training on the AusLaw Citation Benchmark

   Venue/year: Artificial Intelligence and Law, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10506-026-09506-9
   Project: https://auslawbench.github.io/
   Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/auslawbench/AusLaw-Citation-Benchmark
   Models: https://huggingface.co/auslawbench
   Scopus "Cited by" value in export: 1

   Why use:
   - Provides 55k legal citation examples, models, and notebook code.
   - Compares prompting, sparse/dense retrieval, fine-tuning, and reranking.
   - Establishes that retrieval and domain adaptation matter more than merely using
     a larger standalone generative model.

   Vietnamese extension:
   Predict the exact Vietnamese statute/article/circular cited by a passage, then
   evaluate citation accuracy separately from answer fluency.

3. [A] HCLeK: Hierarchical Compression of Legal Knowledge for
   Retrieval-Augmented Generation

   Venue/year: CIKM, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3746252.3760925
   Code: https://github.com/fupanY/HCLeK

   Why use:
   - Targets long, hierarchical legal documents rather than treating every chunk as
     an unrelated flat passage.
   - Useful for Vietnamese documents organized into parts, chapters, sections,
     articles, and clauses.
   - Provides a concrete compression/retrieval method to compare against naive
     fixed-size chunking.

4. [A/B] PAKTON: A Multi-Agent Framework for Question Answering in Long Legal
   Agreements

   Venue/year: EMNLP, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.403
   Code/system/evaluation:
   https://github.com/petrosrapto/PAKTON

   Why use:
   - Fully open-source, end-to-end contract analysis with on-premise deployment.
   - Includes retrieval, classification, qualitative evaluation, human evaluation,
     and LegalBenchRAG/ContractNLI experiments.
   - Useful if the team chooses contracts or agreements instead of statutory QA.

   Availability warning:
   The repository notes that some deployed-version components are still being added.
   Verify the exact reproduction path before committing the thesis to its full
   multi-agent architecture.

5. [A] ScenarioBench: Trace-Grounded Compliance Evaluation for Text-to-SQL
   and RAG

   Venue/year: CASCON, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/CASCON66301.2025.00111
   Artifact/code:
   https://github.com/ShabnamAtf/ScenarioBench/releases/tag/v0.1.0

   Why use:
   - Requires answers/decisions to cite clause IDs from the retrieved policy canon.
   - Evaluates decision accuracy, trace completeness/correctness/order, retrieval,
     SQL result equivalence, latency, policy coverage, and hallucination rate.
   - Provides a useful design pattern for falsifiable, audit-ready legal RAG.

   Limitation:
   The seed suite is small. Use its evaluation design, but create a larger,
   expert-reviewed Vietnamese set.

6. [A, public dataset] BR-TaxQA-R: A Dataset for Question Answering with
   References for Brazilian Personal Income Tax Law, Including Case Law

   Venue/year: 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-15987-8_14
   Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15916
   Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/unicamp-dl/BR-TaxQA-R

   Why use:
   - Contains 715 official tax questions enriched with statutory norms and case law.
   - Demonstrates a practical schema for QA with references.
   - Compares segmentation/RAG configurations with commercial search assistants.
   - Particularly useful as a template for constructing a bounded Vietnamese tax,
     labor, education, or administrative-law dataset.

Minimum defensible legal-RAG experiment
--------------------------------------
- Scope: one Vietnamese legal area, not all Vietnamese law.
- Corpus: official documents with hierarchy, source URL, issue date, effective date,
  amendment/supersession status, and clause/article identifiers.
- Baselines: BM25; dense retrieval; hybrid retrieval; hybrid plus reranker.
- RAG variants: retrieval-only, naive RAG, and citation-constrained RAG.
- Metrics: Recall@5/10, MRR/nDCG, exact article match, citation precision, citation
  entailment/faithfulness, answer correctness, temporal correctness, latency, cost.
- Evaluation set: at least 100 questions with expert-reviewed answer passages.
- Safety: research assistance only; every answer must expose its sources and warning.


PART 4: CURRICULUM-GROUNDED EDUCATIONAL RAG/TUTORING
====================================================

Recommended starting combination
--------------------------------
Use LAMB if the goal is a deployable HCMUS/Moodle learning assistant. Use CyberBOT
if the goal is a smaller research pipeline with ontology validation, or EduCraft if
the goal is multimodal lecture-material generation. Use the admissions paper's
evaluation matrix even when implementing one of the open systems.

1. [A/B, recommended deployable system] LAMB: An Open-Source Software
   Framework to Create Artificial Intelligence Assistants Deployed and Integrated
   into Learning Management Systems

   Authors: Marc Alier, Juanan Pereira, Francisco Jose Garcia-Penalvo,
   Maria Jose Casan, Jose Cabre
   Venue/year: Computer Standards & Interfaces, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csi.2024.103940
   Code: https://github.com/Lamb-Project/lamb
   Project/documentation: https://lamb-project.org/en/
   Scopus "Cited by" value in export: 46

   Why it is the best working-system starting point:
   - Mature open-source web application with active development.
   - Ingests educational documents, performs RAG, and produces citations.
   - Supports local models, self-hosting, Moodle/LTI integration, privacy controls,
     testing/debugging, and multilingual UI.

   Research warning:
   Deploying LAMB alone is not a thesis contribution. Add a controlled retrieval,
   pedagogical, multilingual, or evaluation innovation and compare it scientifically.

2. [A] CyberBOT: Ontology-Grounded Retrieval Augmented Generation for
   Reliable Cybersecurity Education

   Venue/year: 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3746252.3761478
   Code/data: https://github.com/rccrdmr/CyberBOT

   Why use:
   - End-to-end backend, frontend, dataset, document chunking, FAISS indexing, and
     QA-pair embedding are public.
   - Combines course RAG with ontology-based answer validation.
   - Handles follow-up questions through intent rewriting.
   - Naturally connects the education and cybersecurity recommendations.

   Thesis extension:
   Replace the cybersecurity course with an HCMUS subject, compare standard RAG
   against ontology-validated RAG, and measure unsupported-claim reduction.

3. [A/B] EduCraft: A System for Generating Pedagogical Lecture Scripts from
   Long-Context Multimodal Presentations

   Venue/year: 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3746252.3761578
   Code: https://github.com/wyuc/EduCraft

   Why use:
   - Provides multimodal slide processing, VLM and caption-plus-LLM workflows,
     optional RAG, prompt templates, evaluation scripts, and local-model support.
   - Suitable for a thesis on grounded lecture scripts or learning materials rather
     than conversational tutoring.

   Availability warning:
   The code is runnable and MIT-licensed, but the evaluation presentations are not
   packaged as a large standard benchmark. Plan to create a local evaluation corpus.

4. [A/B] KAG: A Scalable Knowledge-Augmented Generation System for
   Educational Content Management

   Venue/year: 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/FLLM67465.2025.11391105
   Code/resources: https://github.com/Hadi-Hsn/KAG

   Why use:
   - Public implementation of a document-ingestion and educational retrieval system.
   - Focuses on source attribution, scalable content management, and semantic access.
   - Useful as an engineering baseline if the team chooses a campus knowledge hub
     rather than a pedagogically adaptive tutor.

5. [C, recommended experimental design] Design and Performance Evaluation of
   LLM-Based RAG Pipelines for Chatbot Services in International Student Admissions

   Venue/year: Electronics, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics14153095
   Scopus "Cited by" value in export: 7

   Why read:
   - Compares recursive versus semantic chunking.
   - Compares dense, MMR, hybrid, multi-query, and multi-vector retrieval.
   - Uses both LLM-generated and human-authored QA references.
   - Reports RAGAS metrics, classic text metrics, confidence intervals, and latency.

   Availability warning:
   No official public code repository was verified, and institutional source
   documents are available only on request. Reuse the evaluation design on HCMUS
   public course or admissions documents.

6. [C] Optimizing RAG in Programming Education: A Comparative Study of Models
   and Strategies

   Venue/year: IEEE Access, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3629843

   Why read:
   - Directly relevant to a computer-science student team and programming courses.
   - Compares commercial/open models, retrieval design, reranking, and prompting.
   - Reports reranking as especially helpful for lightweight open models.
   - Provides a natural thesis direction: a tutor that gives grounded hints and code
     feedback without leaking full solutions.

Minimum defensible education experiment
---------------------------------------
- Course: one subject such as Database Systems or Introductory Programming.
- Data: instructor-approved notes/slides/assignments plus a held-out QA set.
- Baselines: plain LLM; naive RAG; reranked RAG; proposed pedagogy- or
  ontology-constrained RAG.
- Retrieval metrics: Recall@k, MRR, nDCG.
- Generation metrics: correctness, faithfulness/citation accuracy, relevance,
  solution-leakage rate, latency, and cost.
- Educational evaluation: blinded instructor rubric plus a small approved pre/post
  test or misconception-correction study.
- Student data must be anonymized; obtain required institutional and participant
  approval before a user study.


FINAL PRIORITY AND THESIS-SCOPE ADVICE
======================================

1. Secure RAG
   Best reproducibility and clearest objective evaluation. The practical first step is
   to reproduce PoisonedRAG and RAGDefender on a small public QA subset, then add a
   cross-dataset or Vietnamese generalization experiment.

2. Vietnamese legal RAG
   Strongest local relevance. Begin with LexRAG's modular toolkit and AusLaw's
   citation-focused evaluation, then construct one narrow expert-reviewed Vietnamese
   corpus and question set.

3. Educational RAG
   Easiest to demonstrate to real users. Begin with LAMB or CyberBOT, but make the
   thesis contribution a measured retrieval/pedagogical improvement rather than the
   chatbot interface itself.

Avoid "we built a chatbot" as the research claim. A defensible contribution should be
one or more of:
- a public, carefully documented Vietnamese dataset or benchmark;
- a new retrieval, reranking, defense, or validation method;
- a reproducible failure-mode analysis;
- a multilingual or low-resource evaluation;
- or a controlled user study that measures outcomes beyond user satisfaction.

SCIENCEDIRECT DOMAIN RANKING AND RECOMMENDED PAPER SHORTLIST
===========================================================

Source directory:
explore/ScienceDirect/

Prepared: 2026-07-29

PURPOSE
-------
This file applies the same analysis used for the Springer and Scopus exports to the
ScienceDirect BibTeX collection. It:

1. identifies the ten most discussed application domains;
2. evaluates whether each domain is executable by a computer-science capstone team;
3. recommends three thesis directions; and
4. lists ScienceDirect papers to read or use as baselines, prioritizing papers with
   verified public code, data, demos, or reproducible evaluation tasks.

IMPORTANT SOURCE NOTE
---------------------
Every numbered paper in this file appears in one of the supplied ScienceDirect
BibTeX files. Internet search was used only to verify repositories, project pages,
datasets, and implementation availability. No externally discovered paper was added
to the numbered shortlist.

Dataset overview:
- 24 BibTeX files were inspected.
- 2,310 unique records were parsed; no duplicate DOI/title records were detected.
- 860 records are from 2025 and 1,450 are from 2026.
- The largest journals in the export include Expert Systems with Applications,
  Knowledge-Based Systems, Advanced Engineering Informatics, Information Processing
  & Management, Neurocomputing, and Journal of Systems and Software.

Artifact labels:
[A] Strong executable baseline: verified public code plus useful data or evaluation.
[B] Runnable system/demo or partially released artifact; reproduction needs extra work.
[C] Read/method baseline: valuable design, but no complete public repository verified.

Always inspect repository, dataset, and model licenses before reuse or redistribution.


PART 1: TOP 10 MOST DISCUSSED APPLICATION DOMAINS
=================================================

Method
------
The BibTeX files do not contain a single controlled application-domain field.
Domains were estimated with high-precision keyword rules over titles and author
keywords. The categories are multi-label: a paper about secure medical AI can count
under both healthcare and cybersecurity. Counts therefore must not be added together.

"Most discussed" below means most frequently represented in this supplied export.
It does not mean highest citation count or the largest field across all of
ScienceDirect.

Rank  Domain                                      Papers  Share   Feasibility
----  ------------------------------------------  ------  ------  -----------
1     Healthcare and biomedicine                    459    19.9%   2/5
2     Engineering, manufacturing and industrial     265    11.5%   4/5*
3     Business, management and finance               252    10.9%   3/5
4     Environment, climate and sustainability        168     7.3%   4/5
5     Education and learning                         158     6.8%   5/5
6     Law, policy and public administration           140     6.1%   5/5
7     Cybersecurity, privacy and safety               131     5.7%   5/5
8     Transport, mobility and maritime                122     5.3%   3/5
9     Energy and power systems                        102     4.4%   3/5
10    Software engineering and programming             84     3.6%   5/5

*Industrial feasibility is 4/5 only for document-, code-, simulation-, or public-
dataset tasks. It drops to 2/5 when a thesis requires factory hardware, proprietary
logs, or real production deployment.

Domain-by-domain interpretation
-------------------------------

1. Healthcare and biomedicine
   This is by far the most represented domain, but it is not the best default
   capstone choice. Clinical credibility may require protected data, ethics approval,
   medical expertise, careful safety evaluation, and prospective validation.

2. Engineering, manufacturing and industrial
   SELECTED, with a narrow scope. The export contains public code for maintenance
   RAG, rotating-machine health management, CAD interaction, and manufacturing data
   interoperability. Avoid hardware-dependent or private-factory experiments.

3. Business, management and finance
   Common but broad. Many convincing projects require private customer, transaction,
   company, or HR data. Choose it only with a real data partner or a strong public
   benchmark and a precise decision task.

4. Environment, climate and sustainability
   Public environmental data and strong local relevance make this promising.
   However, the export mixes environmental science, forecasting, remote sensing,
   policy, and engineering, so a team must narrow the task before choosing methods.

5. Education and learning
   Very feasible technically. Its main complication is rigorous learning evaluation:
   a strong tutoring thesis often needs instructors, consent, a student study, and
   outcomes beyond chatbot satisfaction.

6. Law, policy and public administration
   SELECTED. The export contains a paper specifically evaluating three Vietnamese
   legal datasets, plus open legal prompting, clause-routing, and policy-RAG systems.
   Public legal documents and exact retrieval/citation metrics fit a capstone well.

7. Cybersecurity, privacy and safety
   Strong public benchmarks and objective metrics make it executable. It remains an
   excellent alternative, especially for secure RAG, but the separate Scopus
   shortlist already contains a deeper attack/defense baseline collection.

8. Transport, mobility and maritime
   Simulation can be feasible, but real autonomy, aviation, or maritime deployment
   is not. A team choosing this domain should use SUMO or another public simulator.

9. Energy and power systems
   Important but frequently dependent on grid, building, battery, or plant data and
   specialist modeling. It is viable through public time-series or document QA only.

10. Software engineering and programming
   SELECTED. Although it ranks tenth, it has the best capstone execution profile:
   repositories and logs are public, tests provide automatic ground truth, and the
   export includes multiple released implementations.

Final three ScienceDirect recommendations
-----------------------------------------

1. Citation- and time-grounded Vietnamese legal retrieval/RAG.
2. Public-data industrial knowledge and maintenance assistants.
3. Repository-grounded software-engineering automation.


PART 2: CITATION- AND TIME-GROUNDED VIETNAMESE LEGAL RETRIEVAL
==============================================================

Recommended thesis shape
------------------------
The most defensible version is not "a chatbot for all Vietnamese law." Choose one
bounded area such as labor, tax, education, or administrative procedures. Build
version-aware article retrieval first; only add answer generation after retrieval
and citations are reliable.

Best starting combination:
- QCATune for Vietnamese legal embedding adaptation;
- ClauseRoute for colloquial-query-to-formal-clause routing;
- the document-splitter study for a simple, reproducible preprocessing baseline;
- AT-RAFT for reference-bearing policy answers.

1. [C, essential Vietnamese baseline] A fine-tuning framework based on
   question, context, and answer relationships for enhancing legal information
   retrieval

   Method: QCATune
   Journal/year: Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engappai.2025.111570
   ScienceDirect:
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0952197625015726

   Why this should be the team's first read:
   - It is directly about low-resource Vietnamese legal retrieval.
   - It evaluates three Vietnamese law datasets, including ViBidLaw.
   - It trains embeddings from question-context, question-answer, and
     answer-context relationships using synthetic examples.
   - It reports improvements in MAP@k across ten embedding models.
   - It is a much closer baseline than adapting only English or Chinese legal QA.

   Artifact warning:
   No complete public repository was verified. Reimplement the contrastive-learning
   recipe only after confirming access and licenses for all three datasets.

2. [A, recommended retrieval/routing baseline] ClauseRoute: Risk-Aware
   Clause-Guided Routing for Retrieval-Augmented Legal Reasoning

   Journal/year: Expert Systems with Applications, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2026.133633
   Code: https://github.com/d001121/ClauseRoute

   Why use:
   - Converts colloquial questions into formal draft clauses before retrieval.
   - Combines sparse and dense signals with risk-aware routing.
   - Targets the lost-in-the-middle problem in long legal contexts.
   - Uses a 1.5B adapted model, making a reduced reproduction more realistic than
     training a large legal LLM.

   Vietnamese extension:
   Compare direct retrieval, query rewriting, and draft-clause routing on exact
   Vietnamese article retrieval. Test whether the method helps colloquial queries
   without inventing legal terms.

3. [A, prompt/reasoning baseline] CoLE: A collaborative legal expert prompting
   framework for large language models in law

   Journal/year: Knowledge-Based Systems, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2025.113052
   Code: https://github.com/liboaccn/cole

   Why use:
   - Separates intent identification, legal-domain selection, background generation,
     demonstration selection, and final prompting.
   - Evaluated with seven general LLMs, eight specialized legal LLMs, and ten
     datasets.
   - Does not require parameter updates, so it is a practical orchestration baseline.

   Caution:
   Self-generated legal background is risky. For the proposed system, replace it
   with retrieved official provisions and require clause-level provenance.

4. [B] Large language model for interpreting research policy using adaptive
   two-stage retrieval augmented fine-tuning method

   Method: AT-RAFT
   Journal/year: Expert Systems with Applications, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2025.127330
   Demo repository: https://github.com/renruntao/ResearchPolicy_RAG

   Why use:
   - Combines two-stage retrieval, hard-negative fine-tuning, and a reference-bearing
     response interface.
   - The task is policy interpretation rather than unrestricted legal advice.
   - Useful pattern for showing original context alongside generated explanations.

   Availability warning:
   The verified repository is a demo interface, not necessarily a complete
   reproduction package. Treat the paper as the method specification.

5. [C, preprocessing baseline] A comparative evaluation of the effectiveness
   of document splitters for large language models in legal contexts

   Journal/year: Expert Systems with Applications, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2025.126711

   Why use:
   - Directly compares chunking/splitting behavior on legal documents.
   - Chunking is inexpensive to reproduce and has a large effect on article retrieval.
   - Provides a necessary baseline against more complex graph or agent methods.

   Vietnamese extension:
   Compare fixed tokens, sliding windows, paragraph boundaries, and legal hierarchy
   boundaries at chapter/article/clause level.

6. [B/C, graph baseline] Leverage Knowledge Graph and Large Language Model
   for law article recommendation: A case study of Chinese criminal law

   Journal/year: Journal of Web Semantics, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.websem.2026.100879
   Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04949

   Why use:
   - Builds a case-enhanced law-article knowledge graph.
   - Combines graph retrieval with KG-grounded LLM reasoning.
   - Evaluates exact article recommendation, a clearer target than answer fluency.
   - The paper states that processed data and source code are publicly available via
     its Data Availability Statement; verify the current artifact link before use.

Minimum defensible legal experiment
-----------------------------------
- One bounded Vietnamese legal subdomain.
- Official sources with article/clause hierarchy, issue date, effective date,
  amendment status, and superseded versions.
- At least 100 expert-reviewed queries, including colloquial and time-dependent cases.
- Retrieval baselines: BM25, dense, hybrid, and hybrid plus reranker.
- Method variants: direct query, rewritten query, draft-clause routing.
- Metrics: Recall@5/10, MAP, MRR/nDCG, exact article match, citation precision,
  answer entailment, temporal correctness, latency, and cost.
- Keep a retrieval-only result table even if the final system generates answers.
- Present outputs as research assistance, not legal advice.


PART 3: PUBLIC-DATA INDUSTRIAL KNOWLEDGE AND MAINTENANCE ASSISTANTS
==================================================================

Recommended thesis shape
------------------------
Avoid creating a general industrial agent. Reproduce one public artifact and answer
one measurable question, for example:

- does graph-plus-vector retrieval improve maintenance fault reasoning?
- can a small model grounded in machine documentation match a larger ungrounded one?
- which retrieval signals remain reliable under unseen equipment or fault types?

Best starting combination:
MAKG supplies the closest RAG baseline, RotLLM supplies a public machinery dataset,
and CADialogue provides a clean executable-code evaluation pattern. A team without
industrial expertise should prefer document QA or CAD over raw vibration modeling.

1. [A, recommended industrial-RAG baseline] A Multi-Agent and synergistic
   Knowledge Graph retrieval-augmented generation framework for intelligent
   maintenance

   Method: MAKG
   Journal/year: Journal of Manufacturing Systems, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmsy.2026.02.016
   Code/demo: https://github.com/LAM2360/MAKG_demo

   Why use:
   - Directly combines a knowledge graph, vector retrieval, and multiple agents for
     maintenance decision support.
   - Fine-tunes the embedding model rather than retraining the generator.
   - Provides a public implementation and explicit RAG baselines.

   Reproduction warning:
   The paper's strongest evaluation uses real industrial maintenance data. Confirm
   which data are included in the repository before committing to exact replication.

2. [A, public dataset/model baseline] A unified rotating machinery health
   management framework leveraging large language models for diverse
   components, conditions, and tasks

   Method: RotLLM
   Journal/year: Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engappai.2025.112544
   Code/data: https://github.com/SIA-IDE/RotLLM

   Why use:
   - Releases source code and the Large-scale Multimodal Rotating Machinery dataset.
   - Covers fault classification, noisy conditions, and multiple health tasks.
   - Uses a smaller encoder/projector plus parameter-efficient LoRA adaptation.

   Feasibility warning:
   This is more GPU- and signal-processing-intensive than document RAG. Run one
   component/fault subset before attempting the full training pipeline.

3. [A/B] Agentic active Asset Administration Shell for circular manufacturing

   Method: A4S
   Journal/year: Journal of Manufacturing Systems, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmsy.2026.07.011
   Code: https://github.com/quickhdsdc/aas-mcp-server

   Why use:
   - Grounds agent actions in the Asset Administration Shell industrial standard.
   - Constrains tool calls through schemas rather than allowing unrestricted actions.
   - Demonstrates digital product passports and shop-floor orchestration.

   Feasible extension:
   Evaluate schema-constrained versus unconstrained tool use in a simulator. Do not
   make real machine control part of a student thesis.

4. [A, executable-design baseline] CADialogue: A multimodal LLM-powered
   conversational assistant for intuitive parametric CAD modeling

   Journal/year: Computer-Aided Design, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2025.104006
   Code/data: https://github.com/Hiram31/CADialogue

   Why use:
   - Converts text, images, speech, and selections into executable CAD operations.
   - Releases both code and its task dataset.
   - Uses automatic execution success, refinement, and latency measures.
   - Caching and human-in-the-loop correction provide manageable extension points.

5. [A, non-RAG engineering baseline] Predictive modeling: BIM command
   recommendation based on large-scale usage logs

   Journal/year: Advanced Engineering Informatics, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aei.2025.103574
   Code: https://github.com/dcy0577/BIM-Command-Recommendation

   Why read:
   - Demonstrates sequence-based next-command prediction in an engineering tool.
   - Uses Recall@10 and other objective recommendation metrics.
   - Provides a strong comparison between predictive assistance and generative agents.

   Data warning:
   The paper uses very large private usage logs. Verify whether the repository offers
   sufficient sample/public data; otherwise reuse the evaluation design, not its scale.

6. [C, hybrid retrieval design] Advancing quality control in off-site
   construction with large language models enhanced by hybrid
   retrieval-augmented generation

   Journal/year: Advanced Engineering Informatics, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aei.2026.104381

   Why use:
   - Aligns dense retrieval, sparse retrieval, and knowledge-graph traversal.
   - Evaluates information retrieval, compliance checking, and task guidance.
   - Uses medium-sized open models, showing that method design can matter more than
     using the largest generator.

   Limitation:
   No complete public repository was verified. Use it as an architecture and
   ablation reference after a released baseline is running.

Minimum defensible industrial experiment
----------------------------------------
- One public dataset or fully redistributable document corpus.
- One task: fault QA, maintenance recommendation, CAD execution, or command prediction.
- Baselines: no retrieval, BM25, dense retrieval, and hybrid retrieval.
- Proposed contribution: graph/structure-aware reranking, domain-adapted embeddings,
  schema-constrained tools, or cross-equipment generalization.
- Metrics: retrieval Recall@k/MRR, task accuracy or execution success, unsupported
  claim rate, robustness to unseen fault/equipment types, latency, and cost.
- Evaluate a small open model and one stronger model under the same evidence budget.
- Do not claim real-world safety or production readiness from a laboratory benchmark.


PART 4: REPOSITORY-GROUNDED SOFTWARE-ENGINEERING AUTOMATION
===========================================================

Recommended thesis shape
------------------------
Choose one task whose result can be automatically checked. The easiest ScienceDirect
options are log parsing, commit-message generation, unit-test generation, or
repository refactoring. Avoid a vague "AI coding assistant."

Best starting combination:
SCULP offers the cleanest released implementation and benchmark task. Consensus
Planning offers a second executable code-generation baseline. CoRaCMG gives a simple
project-specific retrieval thesis that can be reproduced from public Git histories.

1. [A, recommended baseline] SCULP: An unsupervised LLM-based log parser
   with self-correcting capabilities

   Journal/year: Journal of Systems and Software, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2026.112935
   Code: https://github.com/lezhang-thu/SCULP

   Why use:
   - Public implementation validated by the journal's Open Science Board.
   - Uses the large Loghub-2.0 benchmark.
   - Requires no labeled demonstrations.
   - Has a focused self-correction component that can be ablated.
   - Supports accuracy, robustness, throughput, and API-cost experiments.

   Feasible extension:
   Test smaller local models, Vietnamese application logs, unseen systems, or a
   confidence gate that invokes the LLM only when a classical parser is uncertain.

2. [A, code-generation baseline] Consensus planning boosts LLM code
   generation

   Journal/year: Information and Software Technology, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2026.108030
   Code: https://github.com/AISP-group/CPCG

   Why use:
   - Releases the multi-agent consensus-planning implementation.
   - Evaluates eight program-synthesis benchmarks and several languages.
   - Uses executable correctness through pass@1 rather than only text similarity.

   Thesis extension:
   Compare consensus planning with retrieved repository evidence and measure whether
   additional agents justify their token and latency cost.

3. [A, structured code-RAG baseline] Geo-FuB: A method for constructing an
   Operator-Function knowledge base for geospatial code generation with large
   language models

   Journal/year: Knowledge-Based Systems, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.knosys.2025.113624
   Code/data: https://github.com/whuhsy/GEE-FuB

   Why use:
   - Builds structured operator-function knowledge from 154,075 Google Earth Engine
     scripts.
   - Combines semantic features, ASTs, frequent operator combinations, and RAG.
   - Offers a concrete example of repository/domain structure reducing hallucinations.

   Generalizable idea:
   Replace Google Earth Engine operators with the APIs of one public Python or Java
   library and compare structure-aware retrieval with flat chunk retrieval.

4. [C, repository-level architecture] RepoAI: Automated code refactoring
   through multi-agent LLM orchestration and retrieval-augmented generation

   Journal/year: Science of Computer Programming, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scico.2026.103477

   Why use:
   - Coordinates intent, retrieval, multi-file generation, validation, and GitHub
     integration.
   - Evaluates multiple pipeline ablations and model families.
   - Uses RefactorBench tasks from more than ten open-source projects.
   - Explicitly addresses self-evaluation bias with cross-family validation.

   Artifact warning:
   No official public repository was verified during this review. Use the paper and
   public benchmark as a design reference, not the first implementation dependency.

5. [C, directly reproducible idea] CoRaCMG: Contextual retrieval-augmented
   framework for commit message generation

   Journal/year: Information and Software Technology, 2026
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2026.108169
   Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18337

   Why use:
   - Retrieves similar diff-message pairs to learn project terminology and style.
   - Evaluates Java, Python, and C++ with multiple LLMs and human judgments.
   - Reports diminishing returns after a small number of retrieved examples.

   Feasible reproduction:
   Mine commit/diff pairs from frozen public repositories. Compare BM25, code/diff
   embeddings, hybrid retrieval, and project-aware reranking.

6. [C, testing baseline] TestLoter: A logic-driven framework for automated
   unit test generation and error repair using large language models

   Journal/year: Journal of Computer Languages, 2025
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cola.2025.101348

   Why use:
   - Combines white-box reasoning with black-box functional validation.
   - Adds hierarchical repair for invalid generated tests.
   - Evaluates nine open-source projects using line and branch coverage.

   Availability warning:
   ScienceDirect reports that data are available on request, and no official code
   repository was verified. Use it as a method/comparison paper unless the authors
   provide the artifact.

Minimum defensible software-engineering experiment
--------------------------------------------------
- One task and a frozen set of public repositories/log datasets.
- Prevent temporal, repository, and test-set leakage.
- Baselines appropriate to the task:
  * log parsing: Drain/classical parser, LUNAR-style LLM parser, SCULP;
  * commit messages: no retrieval, BM25, dense, hybrid;
  * code generation/refactoring: direct generation, RAG, RAG plus validation;
  * tests: direct prompting, test-aware retrieval, repair loop.
- Metrics:
  * logs: grouping/template accuracy, throughput, API calls;
  * commit messages: automatic metrics plus blinded usefulness/correctness;
  * code/refactoring: compilation, tests passed, pass@k, semantic preservation;
  * tests: compilation, pass rate, line/branch coverage, mutation score.
- Include latency, token use, monetary cost, and a smaller local model.
- Run ablations proving which retrieval, planning, or correction component matters.


PART 5: FINAL DECISION GUIDE
============================

Choose Vietnamese legal retrieval if:
- local usefulness and a distinctive thesis contribution are priorities;
- the team can secure legal-expert annotation/review; and
- members are comfortable with retrieval evaluation and document versioning.

Choose industrial knowledge/maintenance if:
- the team wants a domain that is both common in ScienceDirect and less saturated
  than healthcare;
- it can commit to one released dataset or simulator before implementation; and
- members have interest in knowledge graphs, signals, CAD, or industrial standards.

Choose software-engineering automation if:
- reproducibility and automatic metrics matter most;
- the team has no external domain expert or private-data partner; and
- members are comfortable with repositories, testing, logs, or program analysis.

Overall recommendation
----------------------
The safest execution choice is software engineering, especially adaptive LLM log
parsing based on SCULP or project-specific retrieval for commit messages.

The most locally distinctive choice is Vietnamese legal retrieval because the
ScienceDirect collection already contains a directly relevant Vietnamese baseline.

The best balance between ScienceDirect frequency and practical novelty is industrial
knowledge/maintenance, but only when the team first confirms a complete public
dataset and avoids real equipment control.
